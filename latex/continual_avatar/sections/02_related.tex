\section{Related Work}\label{sec:related}

\subsection{Monocular-Video Human Reconstruction}
Reconstructing clothed humans from monocular video has been approached
through implicit representations and parametric body priors.
Vid2Avatar~\cite{guo2023vid2avatar} learns animatable avatars from
in-the-wild video via self-supervised scene decomposition, and its
successor Vid2Avatar-Pro~\cite{guo2025vid2avatarpro} introduces a universal
prior learned from multi-view capture. Single-image methods such as
SIFU~\cite{zhang2024sifu}, ECON~\cite{xiu2023econ}, TeCH~\cite{huang2023tech}
and SiTH~\cite{ho2024sith} reconstruct clothed humans using implicit
functions or diffusion priors to hallucinate unseen regions.
InstantAvatar~\cite{jiang2023instantavatar} accelerates monocular neural
avatar fitting to about one minute. These works establish the feasibility
of fast monocular avatar reconstruction but do not support continuous,
streaming refinement of an already-created avatar.

\subsection{Drivable 3D Gaussian Human Avatars}
3DGS~\cite{kerbl3Dgaussians} enables real-time radiance-field rendering and
has rapidly been adopted for human avatars.
GauHuman~\cite{hu2024gauhuman} trains articulated Gaussian humans from
monocular video in minutes with real-time rendering.
GaussianAvatar~\cite{hu2024gaussianavatar} and
3DGS-Avatar~\cite{qian2024gsavatar} attach Gaussians to, or deform them
with, a SMPL skeleton to produce animatable avatars from a single video.
HUGS~\cite{kocabas2024hugs} jointly models the human and scene, while
SplattingAvatar~\cite{shao2024splattingavatar},
Rig3DGS~\cite{rivero2024rig3dgs}, GaussianBody~\cite{li2024gaussianbody},
SplatArmor~\cite{jena2023splatarmor}, ASH~\cite{pang2024ash} and
PhysGaussian~\cite{xie2024physgaussian} explore mesh-embedded, controllable,
clothed, and physics-integrated variants. All of these are per-video
optimization methods and assume the full sequence is available offline,
which \method{} explicitly relaxes for the streaming setting.

\subsection{Feed-Forward 3D Gaussian Generation}
A complementary line produces 3D Gaussians in a single forward pass.
TripoSplat~\cite{yan2026triposplat}, LGM~\cite{tang2024lgm},
GS-LRM~\cite{zhang2024gslrm} and TriplaneGaussian~\cite{zou2024triplanegaussian}
generate general-scene Gaussians from one or few images.
HumanSplat~\cite{pan2024humansplat} and LHM/LHM++~\cite{qiu2025lhm,qiu2025lhmpp}
specialize this to animatable humans, and PointSplat~\cite{guo2026pointsplat}
targets compact human Gaussians for streaming. These generators offer speed
and generalization but limited detail, which \method{} compensates for
through online continual refinement.

\subsection{Streaming and Continual 3D Reconstruction}
StreamSplat~\cite{wu2025streamsplat}, StreamGS~\cite{li2025streamgs},
LongSplat~\cite{longsplat2025} and QUEEN~\cite{girish2024queen} reconstruct
dynamic 3DGS online from unposed streams, and
StreamME~\cite{streamme2025} simplifies streaming Gaussian avatars.
CF-NeRF~\cite{yan2023cfnerf} and UNIKD~\cite{unikd2022} introduce
incremental learning for neural radiance fields, while
NeRFusion~\cite{tancik2022nerfusion} fuses per-clip reconstructions.
However, these works target general scenes or do not address the
catastrophic forgetting of drivable avatar parameters. Continual-learning
primitives such as EWC~\cite{kirkpatrick2017overcoming},
GEM~\cite{lopez2017gem} and iCARL~\cite{rebuffi2017icarl} have, to our
knowledge, not been applied to 3DGS human avatars; \method{} adapts them to
the articulated, skeleton-conditioned setting.
